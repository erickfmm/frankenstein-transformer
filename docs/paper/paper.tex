\begin{abstract}
Transformer architectures dominate sequence modeling across natural language processing, computer vision, speech, and computational biology, but the design space around them has fragmented. Attention has diversified beyond dense softmax into recurrent and retentive blocks, sparse patterns, gated mechanisms, latent projections, memory-augmented layers, and fast-weight rules; optimizers have expanded beyond AdamW with variance-reduction, memory-efficient, schedule-free, second-order, and low-rank methods. Comparing these ideas under consistent conditions remains costly, because each variant must be implemented, wired into a training pipeline, and maintained separately---a duplication of effort that hinders reproducibility and makes published comparisons difficult to align. We present Frankenstein Transformer, a configuration-driven CLI toolkit (\texttt{frankenstein-transformer}) that unifies model definition, training, deployment, and inference behind a single strict YAML schema. The toolkit covers forty-five sequence mixers across nine families and twenty-four optimizer families with per-parameter-group hyperparameter control, and supports encoder, decoder, Vision Transformer and audio model classes for masked language modeling, causal language modeling, sentence embeddings, image tasks, speech recognition and audio classification. Its contribution is a validated experimentation contract: invalid configurations fail before training starts, every architecture and optimizer shares one operational interface, and the same document drives training, quantized deployment and export, and a schema-generated web builder. The contract is exercised end-to-end in a measurement campaign of 868 configurations (905 CPU-hours) that trains, exports, quantizes, and evaluates every mixer pair, optimizer, and deployment path through the same interface, published as an interactive report with downloadable datasets. We describe the architecture taxonomy, optimizer routing, deployment paths, and integration surface, and defer mathematical and implementation detail to the appendices.
\end{abstract}
